01The game in 60 seconds

Objective

Two teams of five — Terrorists (T) try to plant the bomb or eliminate defenders; Counter-Terrorists (CT) defend sites or defuse. First to 13 rounds wins (MR12); ties at 12–12 go to overtime (MR3 sets). Halves swap sides at round 12.

Competitive specifics (verified): rounds last 1:55; the planted bomb ticks for 40 seconds; overtime is MR3 — first to 4 OT rounds wins the set, with $10,000 OT starting money at Valve Majors (some third-party events differ, e.g. $12,500 at EPIC.LAN). Matchmaking games can end 12–12 as draws; pro matches always play OT to a winner.

The economy

Money is earned per round (kill rewards, plant/defuse bonuses, win/loss streaks). Loss bonuses escalate up to $3,400 — deliberate "eco" rounds save for full "buy" rounds. Reading economies is a core analytical skill: a won force-buy can flip a half.

Utility

Grenades (smoke, flash, HE, molotov, decoy) are bought, not earned. Executing and defending bombsites revolves around coordinated utility — "lineups" are memorized throw spots. Utility usage is a leading indicator of team structure.

Game/mechanics description summarized from the game's standard competitive ruleset; format specifics (MR12, 1:55 rounds, 40-second bomb timer, MR3 overtime) verified across bo3.gg, clash.gg, Escorenews and a live tournament ruleset (EPIC.LAN). See Liquipedia CS2 · bo3.gg · EPIC.LAN rules.

03The professional ecosystem (2025–26)

How competition is structured

  • Valve's open ecosystem: since 2025, anyone can run events; Valve classifies them as Tier 1 / Tier 2 and ranks teams via the Valve Regional Standings (VRS) — an open, points-based ranking computed from results (published on Valve's official GitHub). TWC 2026 is a Valve Tier 1 event.
  • Majors: two per year, run by third-party organizers under Valve's banner — the pinnacle. 2026's second Major: PGL Major Singapore (Nov 25 – Dec 13), first ever in Southeast Asia, 32 teams, $1.25M. Reigning Major champions: Falcons (IEM Cologne Major, June 2026).
  • Invites via VRS: Majors and big invitationals seed teams by VRS — which is why mid-season events like TWC matter beyond prize money.
  • How VRS points actually work (verified): Valve's model combines a team's prize money earned, beaten opponents' prize money and count of teams beaten, plus head-to-head results. HLTV's implementation publishes the formula: a 400-point base plus up to 1,600 points scaled across four factors — bounty offered, bounty collected, opponent network and LAN wins — with head-to-head adjustments on top. Points are age-weighted (roughly a six-month decay window), standings attach to a five-player roster rather than an organization, and Valve's August 2025 update added a minimum participation rule (5 of the last 10 matches) plus linked-stage prize pooling. Snapshots exclude matches from events that had not finished by the snapshot date.
  • Tier-1 circuit: ESL/IEM, BLAST, PGL, FISSURE and independent bookmaker-backed events (TWC is the largest of these).

The transfer market

  • Two "rostermania" windows per year (post-Major summer, winter) reshape the scene — see our tracker for TWC-specific moves.
  • Loans and stand-ins are common (FL4MUS' 2026 loan saga at BetBoom; YEKINDAR's original stand-in spell at FURIA in 2025).
  • Academy rosters matter: Virtus.pro's entire 2026 rebuild is built on its VP.Prodigy academy core — and inherited the academy's VRS points.
  • 2026's superteam era: Falcons (karrigan, NiKo, m0NESY, zonic) won the Cologne Major; BC.Game assembled s1mple, electroNic (since benched), Magisk, Senzu, mzinho.
